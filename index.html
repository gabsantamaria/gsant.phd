Research thrusts.

From kHz to optical · classical and quantum
2.1 / Photonic integrated circuits

Frequency-agile RF sensors beyond the Chu–Wheeler–Harrington limit.

Electro-optic transducers covering kHz to tens of GHz in an ultra-compact footprint that breaks the fundamental limitations of electrically small antennas.

2.2 / Quantum networks

Microwave-to-optical quantum transducers.

Lithium tantalate photonic integrated circuits for heterogeneous quantum computing networks.

2.3 / Precision timing

Fully photonic mm-wave receivers for optical atomic clocks.

Synchronizing remote optical atomic clocks for positioning, navigation, and timing applications.

2.4 / Parametric amplification

A new class of STO-based quantum-limited microwave amplifiers.

Strontium titanate thin films as a paraelectric medium for microwave parametric processes — sidestepping the magnetic sensitivity issues of Josephson-junction amplifiers used by every superconducting-qubit platform today. First demonstration of varactor-based quantum-limited amplification at cryogenic temperatures.

2.5 / Earth observation

Packaged broadband electro-optic dual combs for sensing.

General-purpose RF-photonic dual comb generators for LIDAR, gas spectroscopy, and hyperspectral microwave radiometry.

2.6 / Biomedical

Microwave radiometry for non-invasive thermometry.

Correlation radiometry for 3D spatial focusing and heterodyne architectures for interference cancellation — for non-invasive core body temperature measurement.

The PI.

Gabriel Santamaria-Botello, PhD

Gabriel Santamaria-Botello is an Assistant Professor in the Electrical Engineering Department at Colorado School of Mines, where his group develops photonic integrated circuit technologies for microwave receivers, transceivers, and sensing systems. Prior to joining Mines in 2023, he was a Visiting Professor at EPFL's Laboratory of Photonics and Quantum Measurements and a Research Associate at the University of Colorado Boulder in the Microwave and RF Research Group. He earned his PhD cum laude from Universidad Carlos III de Madrid after completing research stays at the University of Otago, and received his B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Carabobo, graduating first in his class. His research spans microwave photonics, quantum sensing, superconducting quantum computing, defense technologies, Earth observation, radio astronomy, and communications, with work published in journals including Nature and Optica.

Recent milestones from the lab
Featured · Publication

Our work on integrated electro-optic frequency combs in lithium tantalate published in Nature.

With collaborators at EPFL, we demonstrated record-span electro-optic frequency comb generation in a fully photonic chip — validating a hypothesis from our early theoretical work nearly a decade ago. Connor Denney is co-author.

Selected as performer in the $1.65M DARPA HARQ program.

One of 19 teams selected to develop heterogeneous quantum architectures, focused on microwave-to-optical quantum transducers for quantum computing networks. Lab share: $400k plus $130k option. Two-year program with EPFL as prime.

Spinout · Bifrost Electronics

Lab spinout Bifrost Electronics raises over $4M in venture funding.

Founded by PhD student Connor Denney from an Advanced Industries grant, Bifrost has licensed our strontium titanate parametric amplifier technology from Mines' TTO and now employs several former Mines students.

Publication · Under review

Integrated photonic mm-wave receiver with sub-ambient noise — under minor revisions at Nature Photonics.

Demonstration of ultra-low-noise millimeter-wave detection in a fully photonic chip. The microwave structure co-design, led by our group, was key to the unprecedented transduction efficiency.
